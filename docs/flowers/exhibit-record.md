# The Work of Flowers — exhibit record

## Curatorial thesis

A flower is not a still ornament. It is a temporary reproductive machine that changes scale, recruits another animal, and reorganises itself after one successful contact.

The visitor should leave able to distinguish pollination from fertilisation, locate the orchid column and inferior ovary, and understand why a realistic animation is not automatically an observation.

## Experience spine

The exhibit is one scroll-driven real-time scene with five inspectable chapters:

1. **Form** — orbit a Smithsonian surface scan and read the visible flower as an encounter architecture.
2. **Inside** — move a longitudinal clipping plane through the surface while a separately qualified internal reconstruction appears in the same coordinate frame.
3. **Transfer** — a conventionally modelled carpenter bee enters the scene, contacts the column, and carries a pollinarium. The event can be scrubbed and inspected from any angle.
4. **Fertilisation** — the camera uses the stigma as a spatial anchor, crosses a declared scale boundary, and enters an interactive microscopic reconstruction of pollen hydration and tube growth.
5. **Outcome** — the camera returns to the flower while time-compressed states dismantle the display and enlarge the inferior ovary into a capsule.

Scrolling advances the master sequence. Chapter buttons and the range control move to deterministic points on the same timeline; drag rotation remains available throughout. Wheel zoom is intentionally disabled so the page never traps scrolling.

## Evidence policy

The exterior orchid is direct observed geometry. No other animated state is presented as a scan of that individual.

- **Observed surface scan:** source texture and geometry from Smithsonian Gardens accession 2019-0352A.
- **Source-constrained reconstruction:** conventionally authored 3D geometry aligned to the scan and bounded by cited botanical and micro-CT literature.
- **Explanatory reconstruction:** spatial and temporal processes that cannot be observed continuously at one capture scale.
- **Time-compressed reconstruction:** developmental change shown faster than life and without a same-specimen registered time series.

Every chapter carries its evidence label beside the representation and repeats the qualification in the accessible text edition.

## Known representational limits

- The Smithsonian asset is a surface capture; clipping it cannot reveal real interior tissue.
- The published orchid micro-CT reference is not the same species or individual and is used only to constrain a separately labelled reconstruction.
- The bee event describes a pollination syndrome rather than a recorded visit to the scanned bloom.
- Fertilisation spans scales and durations that cannot be represented as one literal camera take.
- Fruit set is not derived from a registered time series of the exhibited specimen.

These limits are permanent parts of the exhibit language, not temporary disclaimers to remove during polishing.
